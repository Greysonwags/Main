# Wisconsin golf course outreach playbook

What you're selling: the outing booking system (instant estimate, instant
reply, staff alert, follow-ups, next-year rebooking, monthly report). The demo
is in `golf_demo/`; the target list is `wisconsin_golf_targets.csv`.

## 1. Why right now is the best time

Wisconsin golf runs roughly May to October. That makes **October through
March the best time to sell**:

- Staff aren't run off their feet, so they answer calls and take meetings.
- Next season's outings get booked in **January to April**. "Be live before
  booking season" is a real deadline that helps you close.
- Your goal: 3-5 courses signed and set up by **March 1**.

**Pricing for a seasonal state.** Many Wisconsin courses won't want to pay
monthly in January. Offer either:
- $1,500 setup plus $500 a month, March to October (8 months), or
- $1,500 setup plus **$3,900 a year paid up front** (about 2 months free).
  Paying up front is better for your cash flow, and owners like the discount.

## 2. Who to call at each course

| Course type | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Independent public or semi-private | **Owner** or **General Manager**. The head pro often has strong influence. |
| Private club | General Manager or Clubhouse Manager. Outings are usually on Mondays. |
| Resort | Director of Sales or Events Manager (they already have staff: a harder sale) |
| City or county course | Golf Manager, then a procurement process (slow; save for later) |
| Management company | Director of Operations (one yes covers several courses) |

How to find the person:
1. The course website's "Contact", "Staff" or "Outings" page.
2. LinkedIn: search "[course name]", or "General Manager golf Wisconsin".
3. Call the pro shop and ask: "Who handles outing bookings there?" Write
   down the name, then ask for that person.

Record what you find in the empty columns of the spreadsheet.

## 3. The order to work the list

1. **Warm leads first:** the course you worked at, plus anyone you know through
   golf. A first client who trusts you is worth more than 50 cold emails.
2. **Tier A+** (management companies): pitch these only after you have 1-2
   results, because they'll ask "who else uses this?"
3. **Tier A** (66 independent courses): this is your main market. Start
   with your own region so you can visit in person if a call goes well.
4. **Tier B** (resorts and private clubs): after you have case studies.
5. **Tier C** (city, county and state courses): last. Selling to
   governments is slow.

Before contacting a course, spend 2 minutes on its website and note **one
specific thing**: Is the outing info just a PDF? Does it say "call the pro
shop"? Is there no outing page at all? Mentioning that makes your message
clearly not a mass email.

(Don't send fake outing inquiries to test their response time. It wastes
their staff's time and looks bad if found out. Their website gives you
enough to point to.)

## 4. Cold email series

Send these from a **separate domain** (e.g. `getfairwayfollowup.com`), not
your main one, so your main address keeps a good spam reputation. Keep it to
at most 30-40 emails a day per inbox. US law (CAN-SPAM) requires a real
mailing address and an easy way to opt out, so include both in your
signature.

**Email 1 (day 1)**
> Subject: outings at {Course}
>
> Hi {First name},
>
> I worked at {course you worked at} and saw how many outing inquiries
> slipped through the cracks in busy season: a voicemail nobody returned,
> a quote sent once and never followed up.
>
> I noticed {specific thing: e.g. "your outing info is a PDF with a phone
> number"}. I built a system for Wisconsin courses that gives planners an
> instant estimate on your website, replies to them within a minute, and
> follows up until they book. It also asks last year's groups to rebook.
>
> One extra outing pays for a year of it. Worth a 10-minute look before
> booking season?
>
> {Your name}
> {Phone} · {Mailing address}
> Not the right person? Just reply and let me know.

**Email 2 (day 3)**
> Subject: re: outings at {Course}
>
> Quick example: a 48-golfer outing with a buffet is around $4,800. If one
> group a season books somewhere else because nobody followed up, that's
> more than this costs for the whole year.
>
> Happy to show you on your phone in 10 minutes. Is {day} or {day} better?

**Email 3 (day 7)**
> Subject: next year's outings
>
> Most of next season's outings get booked between January and April. I'm
> setting up a few Wisconsin courses before then. Want me to hold a spot
> for {Course}?

**Email 4 (day 14, the last one)**
> Subject: closing the loop
>
> I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing isn't right. If outings
> become a priority before spring, just reply to this. Have a great
> off-season.

## 5. Phone script

**Gatekeeper (pro shop):**
> "Hi, this is {name}. Who handles outing bookings there? ... Great, is
> {name} around?"

**Decision maker:**
> "Hi {name}, this is {your name}. I'll be quick. I used to work at {course},
> and I build outing booking systems for Wisconsin courses. Can I ask you
> one question?
>
> When a company emails about a 60-person outing during a busy July
> weekend, how fast does someone usually get back to them?"

*(Let them answer. Most will admit "it depends" or "a day or two.")*

> "That's what I hear everywhere. The planner usually emails three courses
> and books whoever answers first. My system replies to them in under a
> minute with a price estimate, texts you that a hot lead came in, and
> follows up automatically if they go quiet. It costs less than one outing a
> year. Could I show you a 10-minute demo, either Thursday or Friday?"

**Voicemail (under 20 seconds):**
> "Hi {name}, {your name} here. I used to work at {course}. I help Wisconsin
> courses book more outings by answering every inquiry within a minute. I'll
> send a short email too. My number is {phone}. Thanks!"

## 6. LinkedIn

**Connection request (under 300 characters):**
> Hi {name}, I worked at {course} and now build outing booking systems
> for Wisconsin golf courses. I'd like to connect with others in the
> Wisconsin golf world.

**After they accept:**
> Thanks for connecting! Quick question: how does {Course} handle outing
> inquiries in peak season? I'm hearing that slow replies cost a lot of
> courses bookings, and I built something for it. Happy to show you if
> you're curious, no pressure.

## 7. Common objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We already use foreUP / Club Prophet / Chelsea." | "Good. This doesn't replace your tee sheet. It handles outing inquiries before they're booked, which those systems don't chase." |
| "We're busy enough already." | "That's exactly when inquiries get missed. This works when your staff can't. Would you want more outings on slow weekdays?" |
| "It's too expensive." | "What's your average outing worth? ... So one extra outing a year covers it several times. And I refund setup if it books nothing in 6 months." |
| "Send me some info." | "Sure. It's easier to see than read about, though. Can I send a 10-minute calendar link along with it?" |
| "We handle it ourselves." | "How many inquiries didn't book last year, and do you know why? The monthly report shows you exactly that." |
| "I need to talk to the owner / board." | "Makes sense. Could we do the demo with them on the call? It takes 10 minutes." |

## 8. Weekly plan (10-20 hours)

| Day | Task | About |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Research 25 courses: find the decision maker, note one specific thing | 2-3 h |
| Tue | Send email 1 to those 25. Send follow-ups due from earlier weeks. | 1-2 h |
| Wed | Calls: 25-40 dials (mornings before 10 and early afternoon work best) | 2-3 h |
| Thu | Demos, plus more calls | 2-3 h |
| Fri | LinkedIn requests and messages. Update the spreadsheet. | 1-2 h |

**Expected results, roughly:** 50 new courses contacted every 2 weeks leads
to 8-12 conversations, which leads to 3-5 demos, which leads to about 1 new
client. You'll have worked through the whole list by about Christmas, which
is on pace for 3-5 clients by March. Track your actual numbers and adjust.

## 9. Keep your spreadsheet up to date

Status values: `Not contacted` → `Emailed` → `Called` → `Talked` →
`Demo booked` → `Demo done` → `Won` / `Lost` / `Not now (follow up in March)`.

"Not now" is not "no". Put every one of them on the list for a March call.
